Job Market Paper

School Ratings and Enrollment Choices of Limited English Proficient Families

PDF
Abstract

Do school accountability ratings help families make school choices when they face language barriers? Using student-level administrative data from Texas elementary schools and a regression discontinuity design around rating thresholds, I find little evidence that higher ratings affect Hispanic Limited English Proficient (LEP) students’ enrollment. At the highest threshold, non-LEP enrollment increases by 1.9 percent and incoming transfers rise by 1.5 percentage points, although differences in responses between Hispanic LEP and non-LEP students are not statistically significant. Hispanic LEP students appear more responsive in schools with high LEP concentration. The findings suggest that public disclosure of school-quality information may have uneven effects across families facing different information and choice frictions.

Working Papers

Family Responses to School Accountability Ratings

(with Gregory Phelan, Javaeria Qureshi and Steve Rivkin)

Abstract

We use a regression discontinuity design around Texas accountability-rating thresholds to estimate how school-quality information affects enrollment and student mobility. We find that higher ratings increase enrollment primarily through incoming transfers and new entrants, with stronger responses when ratings are more informative about families' own student groups.
