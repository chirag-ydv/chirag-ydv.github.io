Job Market Paper

Do Accountability Ratings Reduce Information Frictions for LEP Students?

Abstract

This paper examines how publicly reported school-quality ratings affect enrollment decisions, and whether language-access barriers limit families' ability to respond to public information. I combine restricted-use Texas administrative student records and school-accountability data with a hand-collected measure of Spanish-language television access. I find a limited enrollment response among Hispanic families of limited English proficiency (LEP) students to ratings alone, with heterogeneous responses associated with access to Spanish-language information.

Working Papers

Family Responses to School Accountability Ratings

(with Gregory Phelan, Javaeria Qureshi and Steve Rivkin)

Abstract

We use a regression discontinuity design around Texas accountability-rating thresholds to estimate how school-quality information affects enrollment and student mobility. We find that higher ratings increase enrollment primarily through incoming transfers and new entrants, with stronger responses when ratings are more informative about families' own student groups.
